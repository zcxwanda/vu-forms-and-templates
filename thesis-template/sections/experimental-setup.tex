\section{Experimental Setup}\label{s:experimental-setup}

The experiments address four research questions.

\parab{RQ1: Conversation quality.}
How does the presence of profile-based resistant clients influence overall conversation quality? Additionally, how does performance vary across different resistance categories?

\parab{RQ2: Diagnostic quality.}
How does the presence of profile-based resistant clients influence overall diagnostic quality? Additionally, how does performance vary across different resistance categories?

\parab{RQ3: Quality association.}
What is the association between conversation quality and diagnostic quality?

\parab{RQ4: Conversation characteristics.}
How does the presence of profile-based resistant clients influence conversation characteristics? Additionally, how do conversation characteristics vary across different resistance categories?

\subsection{Materials}

\paragraph{Assessment Questionnaire.}
All conversations use the adult DSM-5 Self-Rated Level 1 Cross-Cutting Symptom Measure~\cite{apa2013level1}. Its 23 items cover 13 psychiatric domains, including depression, anger, mania, anxiety, psychosis, sleep problems, cognition, and substance use. The assistant presents the same items in every condition, giving all simulated clients the same assessment structure.

\paragraph{Clinical Profiles and Diagnostic Documents.}
The experiment uses five profiles from the DSM5AgentFlow codebase: depression, anxiety, post-traumatic stress disorder, bipolar disorder, and schizophrenia. Each profile contains a target disorder, symptoms, and background information for patient generation. The target disorder is also the expected label for diagnostic evaluation. When producing its final report, the diagnostic agent retrieves relevant passages from a local collection of APA DSM-5 fact sheets~\cite{apadsmfactsheets}.

\paragraph{Resistance Conditions.}
The experiment covers all 12 subcategories in the taxonomy. Each subcategory is evaluated under low, medium, and high intensity, together with an extreme stress-test condition. Low through high use the probabilistic activation mechanism, while extreme activates every non-reframing questionnaire turn and every truth-map-positive reframing turn. Section~\ref{s:methodology} describes the generation procedure.

\subsection{Conversation Dataset}

For each of the five clinical profiles, the dataset contains five cooperative baseline conversations. Every resistant condition is defined by a clinical profile, one of 12 subcategories, one of four intensities, and one of five repeated runs. The complete dataset therefore contains
\[
(5 \times 5) + (5 \times 12 \times 4 \times 5) = 1{,}225
\]
conversations. Each saved record contains the assistant--patient dialogue, clinical observation, diagnostic report, condition metadata, turn-level activation metadata, and evaluation results.

\subsubsection{Conversation Quality}

Four five-point rubric measures are based on the DSM5AgentFlow evaluation framework: DSM-5 coverage, clinical relevance, consistency, and empathy/professionalism. Their mean is reported as the conversation-rubric mean. Question responsiveness measures how directly each patient answer addresses the current item. Information yield measures how much clinically useful information the answer provides; irrelevant detail does not increase the score simply because the answer is long. Responsiveness and information yield are averaged across all questionnaire-response turns and separately across activated turns.

\subsubsection{Diagnostic Quality}

Classification accuracy compares the primary class assigned by the evaluator with the profile label. Text-match accuracy checks whether the expected disorder or a predefined name variant appears anywhere in the report; for example, ``depressive'' and ``MDD'' are accepted for depression, and ``post-traumatic'' is accepted for PTSD. The two measures can differ when a report mentions the expected disorder but selects another primary diagnosis. Diagnostic justification measures how well the report supports its conclusion with evidence from the dialogue. Diagnostic certainty measures how clearly the report commits to its diagnostic conclusion. Justification and certainty use five-point scales.

\subsubsection{Resistance Realization and Response Characteristics}

Resistance fidelity is scored only on activated turns and measures how closely the response matches the assigned subcategory. The observed activation rate is calculated as activated questionnaire turns divided by all patient questionnaire turns. Mean answer length, short-answer rate, and nonresponse rate are calculated directly from questionnaire responses. A short answer contains at most three alphanumeric words. A nonresponse is empty, contains only an action, or consists of a silence or hesitation marker such as \texttt{[silence]}, ``huh'', or ``mmm''. These measures capture different response forms rather than one general resistance score: short answers and nonresponses are most relevant to withholding, while unusually long answers are most relevant to irrelevant verbosity. Responsiveness and information yield capture whether answers remain useful even when their length is similar to the baseline.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}

Each conversation is treated as one data point. The analysis compares the five experimental conditions (baseline, low, medium, high, and extreme) and the resistance categories. Because the conversations are repeated generations from fixed profiles and prompts, the results describe variation within this simulated dataset.

\paragraph{Five-Point Scores and Conversation Measures.}
Five-point evaluation scores and numerical conversation measures are compared using Kruskal--Wallis tests. This test determines whether at least one condition or category differs from the others. Results with $p<.05$ are treated as statistically significant. Epsilon squared ($\epsilon^2$) describes the size of the overall group difference: values closer to zero indicate weaker separation, while larger values indicate stronger separation. Because the overall test does not identify which groups differ, significant results are followed by Mann--Whitney pairwise comparisons. Their $p$-values are adjusted using the Holm method to account for multiple comparisons. Rank-biserial correlation is reported for selected pairwise comparisons to show the size and direction of the difference.

\paragraph{Targeted Behavioral Checks.}
RQ4 first compares response characteristics across the cooperative baseline and resistance categories. It then checks whether the intended response form becomes more pronounced across low, medium, high, and extreme intensity within each non-reframing subcategory. The selected measure follows the subcategory definition: mean answer length is used for vague or minimal information and irrelevant verbosity, nonresponse rate for silence or nonresponse, and responsiveness for the remaining diverting, contesting, and controlling subcategories. Each measure is compared across the four intensities using a Kruskal--Wallis test. The nine resulting $p$-values are adjusted together using the Holm method. Reframing is not included in this targeted intensity check because its truth map restricts activation to eligible questionnaire items.

\paragraph{Binary Diagnostic Outcomes.}
Classification accuracy and text-match accuracy are recorded as correct or incorrect. Pearson chi-square tests are used to determine whether the proportion of correct outcomes differs across conditions or categories when the expected cell counts are sufficiently large. Fisher's exact test is used instead for sparse tables. Cramer's $V$ is reported to describe the strength of the observed association. Pairwise condition comparisons also use Fisher's exact test. For these comparisons, we report the difference in accuracy and its 95\% confidence interval.

\paragraph{Associations Between Conversation and Diagnostic Quality.}
For RQ3, Spearman's rank correlation coefficient ($\rho$) is used to examine whether information yield is associated with the conversation-rubric mean and diagnostic certainty. The coefficient ranges from $-1$ to $1$: positive values mean that the two measures tend to increase together, and larger absolute values indicate a stronger association. Classification accuracy is analyzed separately using logistic regression because it has only two outcomes: correct or incorrect. Resistance condition and clinical profile are also included because both may affect classification accuracy. This allows the analysis to test whether information yield is still related to accuracy after these two factors are considered. Information yield is standardized before analysis. The reported odds ratio describes how the odds of a correct classification change when information yield increases by one standard deviation. The model is fitted once using all conditions and once using only the baseline-to-high conditions, where diagnostic accuracy has a strong ceiling effect.

\paragraph{Additional Checks and Reporting.}
Category comparisons are repeated without reframing because its truth-map restriction produces a much lower activation rate than the other categories. This sensitivity analysis checks whether the category results remain when activation rates are more comparable. All tests are two-sided and use $\alpha=.05$. Error bars in Figures~\ref{fig:rq1-intensity} and~\ref{fig:rq2-intensity} show bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals based on 5,000 resamples. Information-yield quartiles are presented only as a descriptive illustration and are not used as a substitute for the statistical tests.

\subsection{Implementation Details}

Gemini 2.5 Flash (\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash}) is accessed through Google's OpenAI-compatible Gemini API and used for the assistant, patient, and diagnostic agents in every condition. The same model is also used for rubric-based, diagnostic, and patient-response evaluation. All model calls use a temperature of 0.7. During diagnosis generation, the retriever returns at most five relevant documents from the local APA collection. No fixed random seed is used; the five repeated runs capture variation from activation sampling and model generation. Keeping the model fixed ensures that the experimental conditions differ in resistance configuration rather than provider or model choice.
