\section{Methodology}\label{s:methodology}

Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} shows how the resistant-client simulation is integrated into the assessment workflow. The clinical profile and questionnaire remain fixed across conditions. The resistance configuration affects the patient prompt on selected turns, while the assistant and diagnostic agent do not receive the resistance labels. After the dialogue, the diagnostic agent uses the transcript and retrieved DSM-5 documents to produce a clinical observation and diagnosis. Resistance metadata is stored only for later evaluation.

\subsection{Resistant-Client Generation}

For each resistant condition, the patient agent receives a clinical profile and a resistance configuration. The clinical profile describes the disorder, symptoms, and relevant background. The resistance configuration selects one subcategory and one intensity setting.

The selected subcategory is converted into an instruction for the patient agent. The instruction defines the intended response pattern without supplying a fixed answer. For example, vague/minimal information asks for an underspecified but relevant answer, while agenda redirection asks the patient to move to another assessment topic before answering. The wording is generated from the current question and dialogue context.

Each prompt also includes boundary rules for nearby subcategories and grounding rules for the clinical profile. Resistance should change how existing clinical information is communicated rather than add a new symptom solely to display the selected subcategory. A shortened prompt is shown in Table~\ref{tab:prompt-template-example}.

\subsection{Intensity and Turn-Level Activation}

Low, medium, and high intensity primarily control how often resistance appears. Their target activation ranges are 0.15--0.30, 0.40--0.60, and 0.70--0.85, respectively. At the start of a conversation, one target rate $r$ is sampled from the selected range. Before each eligible patient turn, the system decides whether to include the resistance instruction.

For the first eligible turn, the activation probability is $p_1=r$. For each later eligible turn, let $a_t \in \{0,1\}$ indicate activation on turn $t$, and let $\bar{a}_{t-1}$ be the activation rate observed on earlier eligible turns. The probability is then
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_t &= r + 0.8\bigl(r-\bar{a}_{t-1}\bigr), \\
p_t &= \operatorname{clip}\bigl(c_t q_t, 0.05, 0.95\bigr), \\
a_t &\sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_t).
\end{aligned}
\]
The difference between $r$ and $\bar{a}_{t-1}$ moves the probability toward the target rate. The cooldown factor $c_t$ lowers the probability after one or two consecutive activations, reducing clusters of resistant answers. These controls guide the distribution of resistance without fixing the exact turns in advance.

Each subcategory prompt contains three expression levels to reduce repetitive wording on activated turns. A level is sampled for each activation, with a small preference for Level 1 under low intensity, Level 2 under medium intensity, and Level 3 under high intensity. The level guides how clearly the subcategory appears in that response; it is not a separate experimental variable. The complete activation, expression, and cooldown settings are reported in Table~\ref{tab:activation-settings}.

The extreme condition is included as a stress test rather than as a direct extension of the probabilistic low-to-high intensity range. It removes probabilistic turn selection and applies the strongest available expression guidance. For the nine non-reframing subcategories, resistance is activated on every questionnaire turn and Level 3 guidance is always used. Reframing remains limited to profile-positive turns because denial, minimization, and externalization require a supported problem. Extreme therefore represents a boundary condition in which resistance dominates the assessment, although its observed activation rate can remain below 1.0 for reframing.

\subsection{Truth Maps for Reframing}

Reframing is meaningful only when the current questionnaire item concerns a problem present in the clinical profile. Denying an absent symptom would be a truthful answer rather than resistance. Minimizing or externalizing an absent symptom could instead imply a problem that the profile does not contain.

To handle these cases, each clinical profile has a truth map that assigns each questionnaire item a positive, negative, or unknown state. The mappings were constructed before dialogue generation by comparing the content of each questionnaire item with the symptoms and background information explicitly stated in the clinical profile. They are stored as fixed lookup tables and are not inferred by the patient model during generation. A positive state means that the problem is supported by the profile, a negative state means that it is absent or contradicted, and an unknown state means that the profile does not provide enough information. Minimization, externalization, and denial are eligible only on positive items. On other items, the reframing instruction is omitted, while the clinical profile remains in the patient prompt. Table~\ref{tab:truth-map-positive} lists the positive items used in the experiment.

The resistance configuration and the turn-level decisions are stored in the conversation log. These fields are used to calculate activation and resistance-fidelity measures, but they are not included in the prompts sent to the assistant or diagnostic agent.
